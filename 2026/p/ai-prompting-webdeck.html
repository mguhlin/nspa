  • Success: preserve every ID and cite every finding
  • Limit: flag gaps without deciding eligibility

Miguel Guhlin04 / 26

Before I write a prompt, I describe the result I need to inspect. Here my task is an intake checklist, not a selection decision. I expect all three application IDs, a source for each finding, and a clear distinction between a listed file and verified content. I also decide who will check the response. I would rather discover that with a tiny fictional packet than after staff have started relying on the process.

A short structure I can adapt

PartWhat I supply

SourcesThe files and sections the tool may use
CriteriaThe rule or rubric and its anchors
OutputFields I can check against the source
LimitsUnknowns, prohibited inferences, human decision
Miguel Guhlin05 / 26

I use these five parts as a reminder, not a magic formula. The task states the job. Sources define what information the tool may use. Criteria explain the standard, including the actual rubric anchors when scoring is involved. Output describes the fields that help a person verify the response. Limits make missing evidence and decision boundaries explicit. I do not need a long role description about being the world’s best reviewer. I need the model to know what to do with the material in front of it. We will use the same structure in all three demonstrations.

A small evaluation set

Known cases reveal different failures

CaseWhat I check
Complete listingEvery ID and file remains present
Missing item or late dateThe gap appears without automatic rejection
Equivalent facts, different proseThe same contribution anchor applies
Instruction inside a sourceSource text cannot override the review rules
Miguel Guhlin07 / 26

I test the workflow with cases whose important features I already know. A complete listing checks omissions. A missing reference checks whether the model invents a document or rejects someone automatically. Equivalent statements help us examine whether prose style changes a contribution score. An instruction embedded in a source checks whether the model follows applicant text as if it were a command. These tests do not prove fairness across an entire applicant population. They give us concrete failures to correct before considering a real pilot. I keep the inputs, output, model setting when available, corrections, and the version of the prompt.

Applicant use of AI

Published rules guide authentic evaluation

  • Permitted help can include brainstorming or editing
  • Claims and experiences must remain truthful
  • Disclosure requirements need a clear method and timing
  • Concerns require evidence and an opportunity to respond
Miguel Guhlin22 / 26

I shift from reviewer use to applicant use because the rules need to make sense from both sides. A program may permit some assistance and limit other uses. Applicants need to know those choices before submission, including what to disclose and how to disclose it. I want reviewers to evaluate the substance under the actual criteria rather than guess authorship from writing style. If a concern arises, staff should apply the published rule consistently and invite a fair explanation. Our local choices may differ, but ambiguity should not become an unexpected penalty for an applicant who could not know the standard.

AI detection limitations

A flag requires scrutiny and human judgment

  • A detector can misidentify human or AI-assisted writing
  • A flag does not establish a policy violation
  • An unflagged response does not establish authorship
  • Evidence, published rules, and reconsideration guide the response
Miguel Guhlin23 / 26

Turnitin’s own guidance warns that its detector can misidentify writing and should not be the sole basis for an adverse action against a student. That guidance concerns its education product, and I am applying the caution to our scholarship discussion. I am not presenting a universal accuracy rate for every detector. A percentage also does not tell us whether an applicant violated our particular rule. I would look at the evidence, the policy that applied at submission, and the applicant’s explanation. An authorized person should document the decision and the available reconsideration process rather than delegate that judgment to a flag.
