What to keep: A judgment, its evidence, and a reason for keeping or changing it.

Entirely fictional. Program requirements: enrollment confirmation, a goal statement of no more than 150 words, and one service example. Missing evidence requires follow-up.

5. Where does our process need a person?

20 min · Core slides and workbook

What to keep: A workflow with named owners, a peer challenge, and a pause condition.

In groups of three or four, choose one small task for C-101. Return to the quality of trust you named at the start.

  1. Map the route from approved input to an AI draft, an evidence check, a human decision, and a record.
  2. Name roles before the tool, before relying on the draft, and before a decision. Allow seven minutes to map and three to test P4 or P5.
  3. Finish: “We will pause if ...” Name the person who can resolve the concern.
  4. Exchange routes with another group. Each group gets two minutes to explain and receive one question.
  5. Revise for two minutes. What changed because another group challenged your thinking?

Approval covers the actual service, settings, and use. Removing a name can leave identifying details. Keep the first tests fictional.

Protected workflow reference

Write a small task, sketch its steps with named roles, record the other group’s challenge and your revision, and finish “We pause if ...; ... resolves it.”

6. What would a fair response sound like?

15 min · Core slides and workbook

What to keep: A revised question and an accessible response route.

Continue the fictional C-101 case. A detector flags the goal statement. The applicant reports translation assistance. The published practice rule permits translation help and requires factual claims to remain accurate. No evidence of invented experiences is supplied. The enrollment document is still missing.

Published practice rule: Translation assistance is permitted. Submitted experiences and factual claims must be accurate. This practice rule does not require disclosure of translation assistance.

  1. Think for one quiet minute. What concern, if any, do these facts justify?
  2. Discuss the published rule with a partner. A detector flag alone does not establish misconduct.
  3. Draft one message in three minutes. If no policy concern is justified, explain that and follow up on the missing enrollment document.
  4. For three minutes, read and listen from the applicant’s perspective. What feels assumed? How could I respond? You may review silently instead of role-playing.
  5. Revise for two minutes. Name an accessible response method, a locally appropriate response time, a decision owner, and a route for another review.
After drafting: a possible ordinary follow-up

Thank you for explaining the translation support. Our published rule permits that assistance. We still need the enrollment confirmation listed in the requirements. Could you provide it through our approved submission channel, or contact our office if you need another way to respond?

Add a locally appropriate deadline and contact. This example is not adopted policy. Do not request private chat histories or apply new rules retroactively.

7. What will you try, and who will help you learn?

10 min · Core slides and workbook

What to keep: One action, a colleague, a date, evidence of improvement, and a stop condition.

Choose one small change that protects the quality of trust you named earlier. Give yourself three minutes to plan, then each partner gets two minutes to share and ask how the other will learn from the attempt.

  • What action will I try?
  • Which colleague will I invite to question and reflect with me?
  • What date within 30 days will we review it?
  • What evidence would show improvement? What would tell us to pause or revise?
