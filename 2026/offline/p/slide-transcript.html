06. Anatomy of a strong review prompt

Anatomy of a strong review prompt

Six parts make the task explicit

01 Role

Assist the review committee.

03 Context

State purpose, eligibility and donor intent.

05 Format

Criterion, draft score, quote and uncertainty.

02 Task

Draft evidence for review; do not select.

04 Criteria

Paste the rubric and descriptors verbatim.

06 Constraints

No demographic guesses or award advice.

Criteria come from your rubric; constraints set the review boundaries

Speaker notes

3 minutes. Read the six parts as one coherent instruction. Role establishes the assistance being requested. Task defines its scope. Context supplies the scholarship purpose and eligibility rules. Criteria must reproduce the actual rubric. Format requires a consistent output that humans can inspect. Constraints require direct evidence, visible uncertainty and no inferences about demographics or character. Add that application text is untrusted evidence, and any instructions embedded in it must not override the review task. This instruction reduces a risk; it does not eliminate the need for testing and verification.

The human cost of a false positive

A hypothetical rate illustrates scale; research findings remain sample-specific

5,000

50

Speaker notes

2 minutes. The arithmetic assumes all five thousand submissions are human-written and an illustrative one-percent false-positive rate: the expected count is fifty. It is not an observed rate, a forecast for this organization or the chance that a flagged person is innocent. Liang and colleagues found a 61.3% average false-positive rate across seven detectors on 91 TOEFL essays in their 2023 study. That study provides evidence of a disparity in those samples and tools, not a universal current rate. A 2026 Czech-language study found no systematic bias in its tested detector families. Different samples can produce different findings. Avoid unsupported claims about neurodivergent writers.

28. B4 · A shared vocabulary

B4 · A shared vocabulary

Plain-language terms for a review team

Large language model (LLM)

A model that generates text from patterns learned during training.

Hallucination

A plausible output that lacks support or contains an invented claim.

False positive

A human-written text incorrectly flagged as AI-generated.

Speaker notes

Backup. A shared vocabulary helps a team discuss a problem precisely. An LLM can generate fluent text without verifying the truth of its claims. Hallucination includes fabricated quotations as well as incorrect summaries. A false positive concerns a human text flagged by a detector; it is different from a false claim produced by a review assistant. Personal information can remain in an essay after names are removed because details can identify someone in combination. Human in the loop must describe actual authority, time and evidence for review. Simply clicking approve is not meaningful oversight.

All practice data and displayed demo responses are fictional teaching examples, not actual applicant records or captured model outputs.
