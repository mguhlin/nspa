2. Task sorting with a safeguard

Slides 4–6 · Activity 2 · 3 minutes · Pairs · Workbook page 3

Assign ASSIST, CHECK, or HUMAN to each task. Add a specific safeguard and the person responsible.

ASSIST: AI helps organize routine information. A person confirms requirements and exceptions before acting on the result.

CHECK: AI suggests an evidence match or interpretation. A reviewer verifies each quotation, source location, and rubric match before using it.

HUMAN: A person makes the judgment or decision, including awards, exceptions, and appeals. AI does not make that decision.

A safeguard is a concrete check or limit. The owner is the person who carries it out. For example: the program coordinator checks a missing-document flag against the application requirements before contacting the applicant.

Fictional taskMy categorySafeguard and owner

Link an essay quote to a rubric criterion
Decide whether to waive a deadline

Before an upload, both gates must be satisfied: approved use (tool, access, retention, reuse, and response plan) and only needed data (code, excerpt, criterion, and source pointer).

Score each excerpt independently, then compare with a partner. Did your scores differ? Which evidence justified the difference? One calibration exercise cannot establish that a workflow is fair.

7. A flagged essay case conference

Slides 16–18 · Activity 7 · 4 minutes · Groups of three · Workbook page 8

Fictional case: a detector flags an essay. The activities and references agree.

Published rule: Proofreading is allowed. Claims must be truthful. Translation and disclosure are unspecified.
RoleQuestion
ReviewerWhat evidence needs checking?
ApplicantWhat neutral question could you answer?
ObserverWhich published rule applies?

Agree on the next action and what would justify it. A detector can flag human-written text or miss AI-generated text. A score alone cannot settle eligibility. Pause penalties, check evidence and rules, invite clarification when appropriate, and record reasons and reconsideration.

8. A policy drafting lab

Slides 19–23 · Activity 8 · 6 minutes · Small groups · Workbook page 9

Write one usable sentence for each row. Exchange drafts with another group and revise. Mark one unresolved local choice.

FocusMy sentence must specify
ApplicantsAllowed help, truthful work, and when to disclose
ReviewersApproved task and tool, evidence check, and owner
ConcernsEvidence review, clarification, and reconsideration

A new applicant or staff member should be able to act on each sentence. The presentation and companion include sample responses for comparison after drafting.

Compare the three sample responses after drafting

Applicants

You may use AI to brainstorm, outline, or proofread, but you must write your own statement, verify each claim, and disclose the tool and type of help.

Reviewers

Use only the approved service for evidence extraction, and the assigned reviewer must verify each quote and source before the committee acts.

Concerns

Staff review the evidence and published rule, invite clarification when appropriate, and provide a contact for reconsideration.

Local choices: disclosure method and timing; approved service and assigned reviewer; reconsideration contact, steps, and timeline.
