5. Human checks and information protection

Slides 15–18 · Workbook page 6

Before

Approve the use, data, and actual rubric.

During

Verify source quotes and correct errors.

After

Record the human decision and reasons.

Minimize to an application code, relevant excerpt, and source pointer. Before upload, check tool approval, access, storage and reuse, and incident response. Test varied fictional cases, compare omissions and uneven treatment, and have reviewers resolve differences.

6. A flagged essay and a fair response

Slides 19–23 · Workbook page 7

An essay is flagged by a detector, but the student’s activities and references appear consistent.

Choose: reject, ignore, or review the evidence and published policy. The practice response is a careful review without treating the score as a verdict. No additional evidence of misconduct is supplied.

Pause

Stop any automatic penalty.

Check

Review the published rules and actual records.

Invite

Offer fair clarification when appropriate.

Document

Record the human decision and reconsideration route.

Brainstorming, outlining, or proofreading may be allowed by local rules. Invented experiences, hardship, achievements, or references are different. Good grammar is not proof of AI use.

7. Decide local rules

Slides 24–27 · Workbook page 8

Unclear example: “AI-generated applications may be disqualified.”

You may use AI to brainstorm or proofread. Your experiences and accomplishments must be truthful. Follow any disclosure instructions. A detector score alone will not decide eligibility.

This illustrative language is for local discussion. Decide the actual allowed uses, truthful-work requirements, disclosure instructions, and review process before publishing a policy.

Applicant use

Which help is permitted? When must it be disclosed?

Reviewer use

Which tools, people, and tasks are approved? How are summaries verified?

Fair response

Which evidence, documentation, and reconsideration process apply?
