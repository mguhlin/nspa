04. The Balancing Act

Efficiency

Organize materials and flag missing items.

Consistency

Use shared criteria and calibrate reviewers.

Fairness

Check for uneven treatment and allow corrections.

Authenticity

Value genuine experiences and supported claims.

Trust depends on human decisions that remain fair and explainable.

05. Two Sides of One Application

Review staff

Organize applications, match evidence to the rubric, and prepare reviewer summaries.

Applicants

Brainstorm ideas, organize a narrative, and revise clarity and grammar, within the published rules.

Write rules that make sense from both sides of the application.

06. Assist, Check, or Reserve for People

Assist

File naming, completeness checks, and standardized summaries.

Check

Evidence extraction, draft rubric alignment, and follow-up questions.

Human decision

Eligibility exceptions, integrity findings, final selection, and awards.

AI can prepare review information. People remain accountable for decisions.

07. Build a Prompt That Can Be Checked

Purpose

Name one specific task.

Inputs

Use approved, minimized materials.

Criteria

Provide the published rubric anchors.

Boundaries

No guesses about missing facts or personal traits.

Output

Require evidence and uncertainty.

08. From a Vague Request to a Review Prompt

Vague

Read these essays and pick the best applicants.

Structured

Use the supplied application and published rubric. Quote evidence for each criterion. Flag missing or conflicting details. Do not recommend an award.

Specificity supports consistency. Verification still belongs to staff.

09. Rubrics Come Before Model Output
Published criterionEvidence to findHuman check
Community engagementSpecific contribution and scopeIs the evidence relevant?
Learning and persistenceActions taken and progressAre the claims supported?
Goals and plansRealistic plan tied to experienceDoes it meet the rubric?

A rubric should reward evidence of the criterion, not the polish of the prose.

15. Keep a Person in the Loop

Before

Approve the task and inputs. Apply the published rubric.

During

Verify quotes and gaps. Correct errors.

After

Record the reasons. Keep an audit trail.

Document the final reasoning, not just the model’s draft response.

16. Protect Information Before the Prompt

Original file

Name and home address; birth date and income; narrative and references.

Task-ready

Application code A17; relevant excerpt only; criterion and source pointer.

Only approved systems may receive actual applicant data, even after minimization.

17. Security Starts Before the Upload

Approved tool

Check the service, account, purpose, and contract.

Access control

Only staff with a review need.

Retention

Know storage, reuse, and deletion.

Incident plan

Report exposure or disclosure.

If a tool is not approved for applicant data, do not use applicant data in it.

18. Consistency Does Not Guarantee Fairness

Test

Use varied fictional submissions.

Compare

Find omissions and uneven treatment.

Calibrate

Have reviewers resolve differences.

Refine and retest before considering real use.

19. Applicants Use AI, Too

Potentially permitted

Brainstorm ideas, outline a statement, or edit for clarity, depending on policy.

Misrepresentation

Invent achievements, hardship, experiences, or references.

Publish the permitted assistance before the application is submitted.

20. Assess Substance, Not the Writing Style

Experience

What did the applicant do?

Evidence

What details support the claim?

Reflection

What was learned? How does it fit the applicant’s goals?

Ask the same substance-focused questions of every applicant.

21. AI Detection Is Not Proof of Authorship
Actual writingFlagged as AINot flagged
Human-writtenFalse positive: human text falsely flaggedNo flag cannot prove authorship
AI-assistedFlag does not prove authorshipFalse negative: AI-assisted text missed

Do not deny a scholarship solely because a detector flags a response.

22. A Flagged Essay — What Would You Do?
A fictional essay is flagged, but activities and references are consistent.

A · Reject

Treat the detector score as proof.

B · Ignore

Discard every concern without review.

C · Review

Check evidence, rules, and explanation.

A score can initiate a careful review, never decide its outcome.

23. A Fair Response to an AI Concern

01 Pause

No automatic penalty.

02 Check

Review policy and records.

03 Invite

Allow fair clarification when appropriate.

04 Document

Record the rationale and reconsideration path.

Same standards. Evidence first. Opportunity to respond. Human decision.

24. Make the Applicant Policy Readable

Allowed use

Brainstorming, editing, and other support.

Boundaries

Truthful claims and applicant-owned work.

Disclosure

What must applicants report?

Review process

How questions and appeals work.

Tell people the rules before they apply.

25. Compare Policy Language

Unclear rule

AI-generated applications may be disqualified.

Clearer illustrative rule

You may use AI to brainstorm or proofread. Your experiences and accomplishments must be truthful. Follow any disclosure instructions. A detector score alone will not decide eligibility.

Sample language for local discussion. Publish the rule before submission and apply it consistently.

26. Write Staff-Facing Rules, Too

Authorization

Approved tools, people, and tasks.

Criteria

Same published rubric for all submissions.

Verification

Human checks of AI summaries.

Accountability

Record corrections and decisions.
