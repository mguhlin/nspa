I want us to begin with a person, not a system. This is Sarah, a fictional scholarship applicant. Her application is almost complete, the deadline is close, and the portal has stopped working. Our decisions could protect her opportunity or make the problem worse. Every person and situation in this lab is fictional. The questions are practical ones that scholarship teams may need to discuss. We will examine what we know, identify who could be harmed, and decide what to do. We are not competing for points. We are practicing how to make a better decision together before a real applicant depends on it.

Red Team spots the risks

Find what could go wrong.

  • Who could be harmed?
  • What are we missing?
  • What could make things worse?

You are looking for vulnerabilities and consequences. You are not the attacker.

Blue Team plans the response

Figure out what should happen.

  • What should happen first?
  • Who has authority to act?
  • How do we keep things working?

You protect people, coordinate action, and plan recovery. Your plan still needs scrutiny.

We will see the mission in stages, so I will not show all the information at once. First we read the briefing and separate confirmed facts from unknowns. The Red Team identifies risks, and the Blue Team selects a response and explains its reasoning. Each team can request a hidden clue. A clue provides scenario evidence or a question to investigate. A nudge is different: it helps our thinking without adding a new fact. Then I will reveal a complication. We can keep our plan or reconsider it. Finally we compare our thinking with the debrief, including tradeoffs and questions that remain open.
